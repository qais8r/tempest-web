What Fit

by Jeesoo Yoon

I haven’t written much since starting medical school.

Not because nothing has happened, but because too much has.

I abruptly arrived in Rochester on a month’s notice after almost building a completely different life in Spokane. It was a generic, dime-a-dozen Waitlist Warrior story, not worth telling here.

I miss home. I miss Seattle weather and Seattle food and Seattle people, but surprisingly, I don't find myself being as homesick as I feared I'd be (sorry Seattle) because of all the people I met in my med school class (who are amazing and probably going to be my friends for life, some of them). Also, I literally don't have the time to be homesick; I don't even have the time to cry. This is just what life is going to be like for a while because I signed up for medical school I guess.

I've done a lot of things that I've never done before, like digging my hands into someone’s splayed-open thoracic cavity and looking inside Mr. Macrophage's ear with an otoscope and hiking 7 miles (whew) and growing a pound of beans on my balcony. I was scared that Rochester would be too sleepy of a town, but it feels like I have a new saga or adventure to tell about every single day. I feel like I have simultaneously become a completely different person but also have stayed exactly who I've always been. It's really difficult to describe.

People tell me that research matters a lot and people say that research doesn't matter. People say that extracurriculars are crucial for the match and people say that the really good programs don’t care about extracurriculars at all. People say you’d be doomed unless you got honors on all your clinical rotations and people say that getting all honors is unrealistic and unnecessary.

Not much has changed since undergrad or even high school in this regard - I cannot escape the incessant cacophony of do-this-not-that about every single choice I ever make for my career. So it goes. I was completing my 624th Anki card of the day today in the back row of Kendall-Hench when the wise man lecturing the class about wellness and health informed us that one of the top 5 regrets of dying people is having worked too hard.

I got home at 10PM today. I had Costco pizza on the floor of a homeless shelter for dinner. I made $60 before taxes. I held Ben's clavicle in my hand, the sternocleidomastoid muscle still attached and dangling from one end. I silently complained about the texture of my chocolate gelato in a deserted mall while watching someone peel the skin off of a dead man's face via Zoom meeting. I wore a navy jacket and got lots of compliments. I hand-signed "HELLO" and "WHAT IS YOUR NAME" and "WHERE DOES IT HURT" on camera. I reviewed my Otolaryngology case for tomorrow evening. I jealously eyed someone's Chick-Fil-A. I dreamt of visiting Chicago, of returning to Seattle. I said that I might want to be an anesthesiologist someday. I frantically googled how to simplify inequalities because I couldn't quite remember how. I got 50% of the questions right during the class mock exam. I thought briefly about how that’s technically under a passing score of 70% but got distracted by the light turning green and was too busy crossing the road to think about it after that. I brushed my teeth and scraped my tongue. I butterfly tapped to the rhythm of the HVAC. I complained about the cable management system of the head and neck. I took a shower. I read the words of the Buddha. I thought about how beautiful and incredible my classmates were.

And yet, tomorrow is fuller still.
